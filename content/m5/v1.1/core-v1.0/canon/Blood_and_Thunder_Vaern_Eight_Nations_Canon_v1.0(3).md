# N1 — Northreach Confederation

## 1. National Identity

Blood and Thunder | World & Racing Bible | National Profile N1 — Final Canon Review

Before moving to Greenvale, we'll consolidate Northreach into the same format used for the approved Frosthall profile.

The existing Northreach decisions remain intact. This pass brings together its political and cultural identity, visual direction, naming conventions, and racing traditions without expanding the historical background further.

Northreach is a rugged northern confederation of six largely autonomous territories, populated primarily by humans, orcs, and goliaths.

Its people value independence, personal reputation, practical competence, and loyalty to their communities. Territorial rivalries are common, and its leaders are notoriously argumentative, but the confederation has survived for centuries.

Northreach was founded following a prolonged period of internal warfare and a devastating external invasion. Its member territories learned that cooperation was necessary for survival, even if they could rarely agree on anything else.

Where Frosthall is a unified kingdom with established royal institutions, Northreach is a politically contentious alliance whose strength comes from the different capabilities of its territories.

National character: Independent, hardy, competitive, resourceful, proud, and frequently irreverent.

## 2. Geography & Environment

Northreach spans cold highlands, broad grazing country, forested valleys, and exposed borderlands. The six territories are connected by passes, river crossings, and seasonal trade roads rather than a single uniform transport network. Stonepeak's steep approaches favor sure-footed travel; Hearthvale's cultivated lowlands support dense settlement; the remaining territories contribute distinct frontier, woodland, and upland conditions. Long winters, thaw-season mud, and sudden mountain storms shape agricultural calendars, supply planning, and the racing season.

Travel and racing conditions change sharply with elevation and weather. A course that is fast in dry summer weather can become a test of traction and endurance after rain or early frost. These are regional expressions of the game's existing surface, weather, fatigue, and incident systems, not new terrain mechanics.

## 3. Population & Culture

Principal ancestries: Humans, orcs, and goliaths.

These populations have lived together long enough to develop shared regional traditions. Ancestry does not automatically determine occupation, political allegiance, or social status.

Northreach identity operates at several levels: family or clan, home territory, and the confederation itself.

Territorial loyalty is particularly strong, but a common Northreach identity emerges during external conflicts and major international sporting competitions.

## 4. Government & Political Institutions

Northreach is a loose confederation governed through a combination of hereditary clan chiefs, civic representatives, and military leaders.

Its central institutions are deliberately limited.

The High Moot serves as the assembly of the six territories, while the First Marshal coordinates matters of common defense and represents the confederation where its members have authorized collective action.

Individual territories retain substantial control over taxation, domestic law, military organization, and commerce.

Political stability is best described as contentious but durable. Disagreements are frequent, but the confederation itself enjoys broad legitimacy.

## 6. Civic Institutions & Social Structure

Authority begins with households, clans, towns, and territorial councils. Clan elders retain influence where kinship and landholding are central; elected or appointed civic officers administer markets and settled communities; experienced captains command territorial defense forces. The High Moot provides a forum for disputes and collective commitments, but routine civil affairs remain territorial.

Trade associations, drovers' groups, craft workshops, and local racing committees organize much of everyday public life. Hospitality and reciprocal obligations matter, especially in remote settlements where travelers depend on one another. A competitor's home territory is a meaningful social affiliation without determining ancestry or occupation.

## 7. Architecture & Built Environment

Northreach architecture emphasizes durability, local materials, and adaptation to difficult terrain.

Common features include heavy timber framing, stone foundations, steep roofs, fortified halls, enclosed courtyards, and practical outbuildings.

Regional distinctions are more pronounced than in Frosthall.

Stonepeak favors massive stonework and mountain halls. Hearthvale has prosperous timber-and-stone market towns. Ironmarch builds formidable fortifications. Eastford emphasizes commercial buildings and warehouses. Redgrass features broad ranch compounds, while Greywood uses timber construction adapted to woodland terrain.

Visual distinction: Northreach looks regional, rugged, and organically developed. Frosthall looks more architecturally unified and deliberately planned.

## 8. Clothing & Visual Identity

Northreach clothing favors layered wool, leather, heavy cloaks, practical boots, broad belts, and weather-resistant outerwear.

Territorial affiliation is frequently displayed through embroidered patterns, clan emblems, distinctive fastenings, and colored banners.

Orcs, humans, and goliaths generally share regional clothing traditions, with practical adjustments for body proportions and individual preferences.

The national visual palette emphasizes pine green, iron gray, leather brown, weathered stone, and deep banner red.

## 9. Language & Naming Conventions

Primary language: Common Northreach

Common Northreach is the shared language used for trade, government, military coordination, and communication between the confederation's six territories.

Its linguistic character is broadly northern Germanic-inspired, with strong consonants, relatively short words, descriptive compounds, and straightforward pronunciation.

Regional dialects and older clan languages remain influential, but naming conventions are primarily regional rather than ancestry-specific. Humans, orcs, and goliaths frequently share the same naming traditions.

## 10. Racing Culture & Traditions

Chariot racing is extremely popular throughout Northreach, but its traditions differ substantially between territories.

The sport reflects Northreach's broader culture: territorial rivalry, physical competition, individual reputation, and an appreciation for competitors who overcome difficult circumstances.

Northreach spectators celebrate endurance, bravery, clever improvisation, and aggressive driving.

Combat racing is particularly popular in Ironmarch, while Stonepeak and Redgrass emphasize endurance and demanding terrain.

Commercial racing organizations are strongest in Hearthvale and Eastford.
Signature competition
The Six Banners Championship is Northreach's premier national racing competition.

Its proposed structure features six major races, one hosted by each member territory, highlighting their different racing traditions.

The championship provides a rare opportunity for the territories to compete under a common national sporting identity.

## 11. Racing Leagues, Championships & Competitions

**Six Banners Championship.** Northreach's principal interterritorial competition brings together entries representing the six territories. Its prestige rests on performing across contrasting environments rather than dominating a single home course. Individual rounds may be hosted by territorial organizers, with a concluding title awarded on accumulated championship results.

**Territorial meets.** Each territory runs its own seasonal races and open events, ranging from settlement ovals to rugged endurance courses. Independent stables can build reputations locally before seeking Six Banners entry. Local rules, entry standards, and prize arrangements are administered by event organizers; they do not create a separate simulation ruleset.

**International entries.** Northreach teams may enter Sundaran competitions and receive visiting racers at suitable major events. The Six Banners title remains a Northreach competition.

## 12. Signature Draft Beasts & Husbandry

Northreach's traditional signature racing beast is the **Storm Elk (Hroven)**, a massive, shaggy highland elk with broad antlers, powerful shoulders, and sure-footed legs. Highland breeders value stamina, cold-weather resilience, traction, and composure over difficult ground. Stonepeak Holds and Greywood Territory are particularly associated with Storm Elk husbandry. Racing lines may select for more manageable antler spread, while ceremonial animals may retain impressive full crowns. Storm Elk are a regional specialty, not a required or exclusive racing species.

Northreach has **no nationally mandated or exclusive draft species**. Its racing culture favors hardy working stock drawn from established regional breeding traditions. Highland stables value sure-footed, powerful teams; valley breeders prize reliable acceleration and responsiveness; frontier handlers emphasize stamina and recovery. These are husbandry priorities and visual distinctions rather than new beast classes.

Breeders commonly maintain records of lineage, temperament, injury history, and performance on particular surfaces. A racing unit uses the established two-beast team configuration, with individual condition and fatigue tracked under the existing simulation. Regional breeds and named bloodlines may be cataloged later without changing this national profile.

## 13. Chariot Design & Racing Equipment

Northreach chariots generally emphasize rugged construction, field repairability, and durability.

Typical features include substantial timber frames, iron reinforcement, prominent wheels, protective side panels, and visible harness systems.

Regional engineering preferences vary.

Stonepeak favors durable endurance vehicles, Ironmarch emphasizes combat protection, Eastford prefers lighter racing designs, and Redgrass favors equipment suitable for long-distance travel.

These are cultural preferences, not exclusive vehicle classes or mechanical bonuses.

## 14. Drivers & Crew

Northreach drivers commonly wear reinforced leather jackets, padded protective equipment, bracers, heavy gloves, sturdy boots, and practical racing helmets.

Team markings are prominent, often incorporating territorial colors, clan insignia, or sponsor symbols.

Ironmarch drivers may favor heavier protection, while Eastford teams often present more polished commercial uniforms.

Stable crews wear practical work clothing, reinforced aprons, and recognizable team accessories.

Northreach racers should resemble professional competitors from a rugged frontier culture, not generic barbarian warriors.

# N2 — Frosthall Kingdom

## 1. National Identity

Blood and Thunder — World & Racing Bible | National Profile N2 | Final Review Draft

National identity: The Kingdom of Two Peoples

Principal ancestries: Humans and Hill Dwarves

Government: Twin Crown monarchy

Defining character: Tradition, cooperation, royal prestige, civic pride, and enduring institutions

Frosthall is an established northern kingdom whose identity was forged through the union of human and Hill Dwarf peoples. Its citizens share a common language, national institutions, regional traditions, and a strong sense of belonging to a single kingdom.

Unlike the loose confederation of Northreach or the guild-oriented Mountain Dwarf civilization of Ironhold, Frosthall is a society of settled communities, agricultural valleys, fortified towns, landed estates, and longstanding royal institutions.

The kingdom is prosperous and generally stable, although its two royal households, noble families, and powerful towns are not immune to rivalry, ambition, or political intrigue.

Its racing culture reflects the same character: prestigious, competitive, tradition-conscious, and supported by established sporting institutions.

## 2. Geography & Environment

Frosthall occupies a temperate-to-cold northern region of Vaern characterized by rolling hills, fertile valleys, wooded uplands, rivers, lakes, and a more mountainous frontier.

Winters can be severe, particularly in the northern uplands, but much of Frosthall supports productive agriculture and established towns.

This distinction is important: Frosthall is not primarily a frozen mountain kingdom. Its landscape is closer to a northern European kingdom of farms, market towns, forests, and fortified cities.

Its settlements are generally older, more permanent, and more densely interconnected than those of Northreach.

## 3. Population & Culture

Humans and Hill Dwarves are the two founding peoples of Frosthall.

Their long coexistence has created a shared national culture. Both participate throughout society as farmers, merchants, soldiers, nobles, craftspeople, officials, sailors, and racing professionals.

Hill Dwarves retain ancestral customs, family traditions, and some distinctive naming practices, but they do not constitute a separate guild-dominated society.

A Frosthall Hill Dwarf is culturally much closer to a neighboring Frosthall human than to a Mountain Dwarf from Ironhold.
Cultural characteristics
- Respect for inherited institutions and established traditions.
- Strong family and community ties.
- Pride in personal reputation, reliability, and professional competence.
- Appreciation for hospitality, seasonal celebrations, and public festivals.
- A competitive streak that sometimes becomes national self-importance.
Frosthall citizens generally see themselves as Frosthallers first, although ancestry, family, town, and regional loyalties remain important.

## 4. Government & Political Institutions

Frosthall is governed by two hereditary royal houses whose partnership forms the foundation of the kingdom.

| Royal institution | Traditional association |
| --- | --- |
| Crown of the Vale | Human founding dynasty |
| Crown of the Hearth | Hill Dwarf founding dynasty |

Both monarchs possess equal sovereign standing and jointly govern the kingdom.

The crowns represent the historical partnership of the founding peoples, not separate governments for humans and dwarves. Both monarchs rule all Frosthall citizens.

Noble houses, local councils, chartered towns, and royal officials exercise influence within this arrangement.

The kingdom's political system is old and occasionally cumbersome, but it has endured for centuries.

National motto: Two Crowns. One Kingdom.

The central national symbol is two interlocking crowns above a shared shield.

## 5. Territories, Domains & Provinces

Frosthall is administered through **royal districts and chartered towns**, not separate kingdoms for each founding people. Both crowns exercise authority throughout the realm; there is no human half and dwarf half of the country. District officials manage roads, taxation, courts, and local levies under the kingdom's shared institutions.

The royal capital at **Highhall** is the common seat of national government. Other established Frosthall locations function as regional centers with their own markets, local councils, and racing associations. District boundaries and additional place names remain open for future mapping; they should follow the kingdom's valleys, river corridors, wooded uplands, and mountain approaches rather than ancestry-based borders.

## 6. Civic Institutions & Social Structure

The Twin Crown is supported by a professional royal administration and a network of district courts, chartered town councils, trade associations, and local landholding institutions. Officials serve the kingdom as a whole, even when their office is traditionally associated with one founding crown. Major royal acts depend on the joint constitutional partnership already established for the monarchy.

Human and Hill Dwarf families participate throughout the same civic and commercial institutions. Apprenticeship, military service, guild membership, and town citizenship are shared paths to status. Public ceremonies frequently display both royal emblems together; local rivalries are regional or institutional rather than a division of the population into two political nations.

## 7. Architecture & Built Environment

Frosthall architecture is unified, practical, and substantial, reflecting generations of shared human and Hill Dwarf building traditions.

The dominant materials are dressed stone, timber, slate, brick, and plaster.
Architectural characteristics

| Building type | Appearance |
| --- | --- |
| Rural homes | Stone cottages, timber-framed houses, steep roofs, substantial hearths |
| Market towns | Narrow streets, stone foundations, multi-story residences, public squares |

| Noble estates | Walled manor houses, gardens, courtyards, substantial keeps |
| Civic buildings | Broad halls, stone arches, carved columns, public meeting chambers |
| Racing venues | Permanent grandstands, stone entrances, heraldic galleries |

Hill Dwarf architectural influences are especially visible in broad foundations, low vaulted interiors, carefully fitted masonry, and substantial communal buildings.

Human-derived traditions contribute taller structures, timber framing, steep roofs, and prominent towers.

These influences have blended over centuries rather than remaining separate architectural styles.

Visual signature: Paired towers, twin arches, interlocking heraldic designs, and substantial stone-and-timber buildings.

## 8. Clothing & Visual Identity

Frosthall clothing reflects its established northern climate and shared national traditions.

Common garments include wool coats, fitted tunics, layered dresses, trousers, sturdy boots, cloaks, and embroidered outerwear.

Formal clothing uses finer fabrics, decorative fastenings, patterned borders, and family or royal insignia.

Human and Hill Dwarf fashions are broadly similar, with tailoring adjusted for body proportions and individual preference.
Decorative traditions

## 10. Racing Culture & Traditions

Chariot racing is one of Frosthall's most popular sports, with longstanding professional competitions and strong regional loyalties.

The sport enjoys support from royal households, noble families, prosperous towns, merchants, and private racing stables.

Frosthall racing is characterized by professional organization, established sporting traditions, and pride in the quality of both drivers and equipment.
Racing philosophy
Frosthall spectators appreciate precise driving, tactical discipline, well-trained beasts, and reliable chariot construction.

Combat is an accepted part of the sport, but there is a cultural distinction between controlled aggression and reckless destruction.

This is not a prohibition on violent racing. Frosthall produces dangerous competitors and aggressive teams, but its sporting establishment tends to celebrate skill and composure.

## 11. Racing Leagues, Championships & Competitions

**Twin Crown Classic.** Frosthall's premier racing title is associated with Highhall and the joint patronage of the two royal houses. Its ceremonial identity celebrates the kingdom's shared foundation rather than competition between the crowns.

**Royal circuit calendar.** Established towns and regional racing associations host professional oval and circuit events, supplemented by seasonal country meets. A stable may pursue individual trophies, a season-long domestic standing, and invitations to the Twin Crown Classic. Racing organizations remain distinct from the royal government even where they hold charters or patronage.

**External competition.** Frosthall teams regularly test themselves against Northreach, Ironhold, and Sundaran entrants. Domestic championships remain within Frosthall; international competition uses the host nation's established events.

## 12. Signature Draft Beasts & Husbandry

Frosthall's traditional signature racing beast is the **Crown Boar (Brond)**, a large, broad-chested boar with a thick winter coat, strong neck, and compact, muscular frame. Originating in agricultural hauling and mountain transport, Crown Boars have been selectively bred into disciplined racing lines. Breeders emphasize matched pairs, coordinated pulling, powerful starts, and stability on established circuits. Their weight and temperament can make rapid directional changes demanding. Crown Boars are a cultural specialty, not a mandatory national racing species.

Frosthall does **not require any single national racing beast**. Its breeders favor robust, tractable draft stock suited to cool weather, paved or packed-road circuits, and the kingdom's mixed upland terrain. The agricultural and transport sectors provide an experienced base of animal handlers, while specialist racing establishments select for acceleration, endurance, and disciplined team behavior.

Breeding houses document lineage and performance and often maintain working and racing lines within the same broader species. Two animals form the standard racing team; individual fatigue, injury, and suitability remain governed by the established beast system. Distinctive Frosthall bloodlines may be named during roster and supplier development.

## 13. Chariot Design & Racing Equipment

Frosthall racing chariots emphasize balance, durability, careful workmanship, and refined construction.

Typical features include fitted timber frames, forged metal reinforcements, carefully assembled wheel hubs, and protective body panels.

Compared with Northreach's more rugged frontier vehicles, Frosthall chariots often display cleaner construction, standardized components, and more elaborate heraldic finishing.

However, they are not inherently technologically superior to foreign designs.

- Clearly identifiable stable or royal insignia.
Royal teams often favor elaborate decorative details, while smaller independent stables use simpler, heavily maintained equipment.

## 14. Drivers & Crew

Frosthall drivers typically wear fitted protective jackets, reinforced leather armor, bracers, racing gloves, and well-made helmets.

Professional teams often maintain coordinated uniforms with prominent heraldic insignia.

Royal-sponsored drivers may incorporate decorative metal trim or ceremonial details, while independent competitors favor more practical equipment.

Crew members wear durable work clothing, identifiable stable colors, and equipment appropriate to their roles.

Visual principle: Drivers and crews should look like professional sporting competitors, not uniformed royal soldiers.

# N3 — Greenvale Commonwealth

## 1. National Identity

Blood and Thunder | World & Racing Bible | National Profile N3 — Final Canon Review

National identity: A prosperous, decentralized commonwealth of humans, halflings, forest elves, and smaller fae populations, united through shared civic institutions and mutual obligations rather than monarchy or centralized authority.

Greenvale is known for fertile countryside, bustling market towns, ancient woodlands, and strong community traditions. Its people value independence, hospitality, cooperation, personal initiative, and local pride.

Behind its welcoming reputation lies a politically sophisticated and commercially capable nation. Greenvale's communities frequently disagree, and its citizens are often fiercely competitive, but their shared institutions have endured.

Defining character: Pastoral prosperity, civic independence, cultural diversity, and a familiar but unpredictable supernatural presence.

## 2. Geography & Environment

Greenvale occupies a temperate region of Vaern characterized by fertile valleys, rolling hills, navigable rivers, extensive agricultural land, and ancient forests.

Three broad environments shape its settlement patterns and culture.

| Region | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Heartlands | Farms, orchards, vineyards, market towns, villages, and agricultural valleys |
| Riverlands | Commercial cities, navigable waterways, mills, bridges, warehouses, and trading centers |
| Great Woodlands | Ancient forests, established woodland communities, sacred groves, and fae-associated territories |

The landscape is heavily inhabited and productive. Greenvale's woodlands are important, but the country is not an untouched wilderness.

Regional identity is particularly strong. Residents often identify with their village, city, or woodland community before the commonwealth as a whole.

## 3. Population & Culture

Principal ancestries: Humans, halflings, and forest elves.

Additional populations: Fae peoples and smaller communities with strong Feywild connections.

Greenvale's populations have developed a shared civic culture while retaining distinct regional and ancestral traditions.

Humans are widespread throughout the nation. Halflings have a particularly visible presence in agricultural communities, market towns, and civic institutions. Forest elves have longstanding connections to the Great Woodlands but also inhabit mixed settlements throughout Greenvale.

Ancestry does not determine profession, political rights, or community allegiance.

A halfling from Willowbridge may have more culturally in common with a human neighbor than with a halfling from a distant rural settlement.
Fae presence — Familiar but Unpredictable

## 8. Clothing & Visual Identity

Zarashi clothing is designed for **sun, dust, and controlled ventilation**. Everyday garments favor layered light fabrics, wrapped or draped outerwear, broad collars, shaded head coverings, and practical footwear. Formal attire uses more structured silhouettes, fine embroidery, jewelry, and carefully arranged ceremonial textiles without sacrificing the civilization's restrained elegance.

Court officials and scholars favor ivory, sandstone, muted turquoise, and gold accents; Desert Wardens use practical protective layers and identifiable insignia. Racing crews wear close-fitting, dust-conscious equipment with stable colors and protective wraps suited to Sunhorn handling. In the Dark Deco Retro Cartoon presentation, silhouettes should be clean and angular, with deep shadow shapes and strong readable ornament rather than dense surface detail.

## 13. Chariot Design & Racing Equipment

Zarashi chariots combine **lightweight precision construction** with durable running gear suited to heat, sand, stone, and abrupt changes of footing. Long, balanced lines, refined metal fittings, and geometric protective panels distinguish elite racing vehicles. Ceremonial examples may be richly decorated; working rally chariots prioritize serviceability and dust protection.

Sunhorn harnesses are designed to accommodate long horns, powerful shoulders, and paired-animal coordination without obstructing movement. Stable equipment includes shaded resting areas, water transport, grooming tools, and carefully maintained tack. ## 14. Drivers & Crew

Zarashi drivers are trained in **measured control, terrain reading, and disciplined coordination** with their Sunhorn teams. The culture respects mastery accumulated through practice, but individual competitors may be daring, theatrical, cautious, or unconventional. Professional reputation is built through results, conduct, and long-term stewardship of animals and equipment.

Racing crews typically include a driver, a Saelvar (Sunhorn handler), mechanics, and logistical support appropriate to the stable. Breeders preserve Lorasael pedigree records, while endurance specialists plan water, rest, and route preparation. Both noble-sponsored and independent racing operations exist; neither court service nor scholarly background is a prerequisite for competition.
