# Blood and Thunder — Draft Beast & Racing Stock Bible
## Unified Species and Breed Reference — Canon Catalog v1.0

**Purpose:** Standardize beast anatomy, breed silhouettes, coats, dispositions, and art prompts for image generation and procedural stable/roster content. No numerical performance stats or new gameplay modifiers.

**Authority:** Species identities and nationally associated stock derive from the approved *Vaern Eight Nations Canon*. The three Sunhorn lines (Saelorin, Arusael, Silsael) were previously established. Other named beast bloodlines below are **newly cataloged for v1.0** under the approved hybrid system; they are creative additions to this catalog, not claims about older canon. Horse entries remain descriptive stock styles rather than invented pedigree names.

**Rules:** All racing teams retain two beasts under existing game rules. No beast is exclusive to a nation, ancestry, or occupation. A breed is a breeding/style lineage, not a new species or mechanical class. Temperament is an individual trait influenced by training and handling; descriptors are not deterministic. Visual style: Dark Deco Retro Cartoon, smooth contours, dramatic graphic shadowing, legible silhouettes, not pixel art.

# B01 — Horse

## 1. Species Identity & Canon Status

**Established association:** General stock; no single national monopoly. **Status:** Core species identity from national canon; named lines in Section 8 are cataloged bloodlines unless explicitly designated descriptive styles.

## 2. General Anatomy & Silhouette

Equine: long face, mobile ears, flowing mane and tail, single hard hoof per leg; deep chest and strong hindquarters.

## 3. Male and Female Appearance

Stallions often have thicker neck crests and heavier muscle; mares often have slightly finer necks and more refined heads. Geldings are common working and racing animals. Differences are variable, not absolute.

## 4. Coat, Coloration & Markings

Bay, black, chestnut, gray, roan, dun, palomino, buckskin, pinto and spotted patterns; mane and tail texture and feathering vary.

## 5. Temperament & Handling

Highly social herd animals; generally flight-responsive, observant and trainable; individuals range from placid to reactive.

## 6. Locomotion & Physical Impression

Balanced four-beat walk, two-beat trot, three-beat canter and gallop; stride and footfall change with build.

## 7. Visual Identification & Exclusions

Long muzzle, mobile ears, visible mane, single hoof; never antlers, split hooves or a draconic tail.

## 8. Breeds, Lines & Styles

### 1.1 — Heavy draft

**Reference / status:** Shire/Clydesdale/Percheron-inspired.

**Visual build:** Very tall, broad, deep barrel and massive hindquarters; thick neck, large hooves, optional feathered fetlocks.

**Behavioral / motion impression:** Measured, steady, powerful visual impression; slow-looking but capable of substantial effort.

**Avoid:** Do not make every heavy horse feather-legged; feathering varies.

### 1.2 — Compact draft

**Reference / status:** Ardennes/Suffolk Punch-inspired.

**Visual build:** Shorter, exceptionally deep and rounded, broad forehead, powerful shoulders, comparatively short legs.

**Behavioral / motion impression:** Solid, dependable and grounded silhouette.

**Avoid:** Not a pony; adult mass and bone remain considerable.

### 1.3 — Sprint racer

**Reference / status:** Thoroughbred-inspired.

**Visual build:** Tall, long-legged, narrow waist, deep chest, lean muscle, long sloping shoulder and fine head.

**Behavioral / motion impression:** Alert, reactive, long-striding presentation.

**Avoid:** Avoid making the body skeletal or fragile.

### 1.4 — Endurance runner

**Reference / status:** Arabian/Barb-inspired.

**Visual build:** Moderate height, compact athletic torso, dry legs, refined head, high-carried tail or distinctive profile.

**Behavioral / motion impression:** Attentive, economical movement; persistent pacing.

**Avoid:** Distinct from sprint racer by compact frame, not fantasy speed effects.

### 1.5 — Light carriage

**Reference / status:** Standardbred/Hackney-inspired.

**Visual build:** Balanced medium build, strong hindquarters, defined joints, substantial neck and high-stepping or efficient trotting gait.

**Behavioral / motion impression:** Disciplined pair-work and controlled carriage movement.

**Avoid:** Not automatically a warhorse or ceremonial animal.

### 1.6 — Mountain pony

**Reference / status:** Icelandic/Fell pony-inspired.

**Visual build:** Short, thick-coated, broad-footed, muscular, low center of gravity, dense mane and tail.

**Behavioral / motion impression:** Sure-footed and hardy appearance.

**Avoid:** Clearly mature pony, not a foal.

### 1.7 — Desert courser

**Reference / status:** Akhal-Teke/Arabian-inspired.

**Visual build:** Long dry limbs, lean narrow torso, fine coat, refined face and sparse feathering.

**Behavioral / motion impression:** Heat-adapted silhouette and light, economical movement.

**Avoid:** Metallic sheen is optional natural coat effect, not magic.

# B02 — Storm Elk (Hroven)

## 1. Species Identity & Canon Status

**Established association:** Northreach signature; nonexclusive. **Status:** Core species identity from national canon; named lines in Section 8 are cataloged bloodlines unless explicitly designated descriptive styles.

## 2. General Anatomy & Silhouette

Massive shaggy elk-like cervid with broad antler crown, deep chest, sloping muscular shoulders, long sure-footed legs and cloven hooves.

## 3. Male and Female Appearance

Adult males generally bear larger antler crowns and thicker neck ruffs; females generally have finer heads and may lack antlers. Racing animals of either sex may be selected for harness-clearance traits; antler variation remains a design proposal.

## 4. Coat, Coloration & Markings

Dark umber, russet, gray-brown, soot-brown; lighter throat and belly, winter shag and mane variation.

## 5. Temperament & Handling

Watchful, herd-oriented, initially wary; patient habituation and pair training create composed racers.

## 6. Locomotion & Physical Impression

Long bounding stride and careful hoof placement on rough ground; broad antlers require harness clearance.

## 7. Visual Identification & Exclusions

Elk silhouette with antlers, shag and cloven hooves; not a moose, horse or reindeer.

## 8. Breeds, Lines & Styles

### 2.1 — Highland shag

**Reference / status:** Named catalog bloodline (v1.0); dense winter mane and thick lower-leg coat.

**Visual build:** Very broad chest, shaggy neck, powerful forequarters, heavy antlers.

**Behavioral / motion impression:** Imposing cold-country working appearance.

**Avoid:** Do not conceal legs in fur.

### 2.2 — Racing crown

**Reference / status:** Named catalog bloodline (v1.0); selected manageable antler spread.

**Visual build:** Leaner legs, tighter coat, compact crown and lighter front end.

**Behavioral / motion impression:** Fast, controlled silhouette for paired harness.

**Avoid:** Keep recognizable elk antlers.

### 2.3 — Greywood runner

**Reference / status:** Named catalog bloodline (v1.0); woodland edge adaptation.

**Visual build:** Moderate frame, dark dappled coat, comparatively narrow antlers.

**Behavioral / motion impression:** Agile and alert presentation.

**Avoid:** Do not add supernatural forest glow.

### 2.4 — Ceremonial grandcrown

**Reference / status:** Named catalog bloodline (v1.0); display-oriented line.

**Visual build:** Deep chest, towering branching antlers, luxuriant mane.

**Behavioral / motion impression:** Regal spectacle; less practical-looking harness profile.

**Avoid:** Do not imply every grandcrown is a race entrant.

# B03 — Crown Boar (Brond)

## 1. Species Identity & Canon Status

**Established association:** Frosthall signature; nonexclusive. **Status:** Core species identity from national canon; named lines in Section 8 are cataloged bloodlines unless explicitly designated descriptive styles.

Oversized domestic boar with broad chest, low center of gravity, thick winter bristles, stout legs, powerful neck, wedge-shaped snout and curved tusks.

## 3. Male and Female Appearance

Boars commonly heavier in jowl, shoulders and tusk development; sows may have smoother head profile and smaller tusks, while remaining robust.

## 4. Coat, Coloration & Markings

Chestnut, black, iron gray, sandy brown, brindle and patchy coats; bristle crest and facial mask variation.

Rapid, forceful strides and grounded turns; muscular momentum is visually evident.

**Avoid:** Keep boar head and split hooves.

### 3.3 — Upland bristleback

**Visual build:** Coarse winter coat, thick neck, compact stature, dark coloration.

# B04 — Meadowstrider (Vellin)

**Established association:** Greenvale signature; nonexclusive. **Status:** Core species identity from national canon; named lines in Section 8 are cataloged bloodlines unless explicitly designated descriptive styles.

Graceful antlerless herbivore with light horse/deer-like head, long legs, deep but slender chest, fine neck, delicate muzzle and compact hooves.

Males may show somewhat thicker neck and shoulders; females often finer through the head and limbs; neither sex has antlers.

Honey, chestnut, fawn, cream, bay, russet and soft gray, with subtle countershading and occasional dapples.

# B05 — Racing Ram

## 1. Species Identity & Canon Status

**Established association:** Ironhold signature; nonexclusive. **Status:** Core species identity from national canon; named lines in Section 8 are cataloged bloodlines unless explicitly designated descriptive styles.

## 2. General Anatomy & Silhouette

Large muscular sheep-like ram with broad forehead, wool or hair coat, thick shoulders, split hooves and curved horn structure; selectively bred for pulling.

## 3. Male and Female Appearance

Rams generally carry heavier horns and neck musculature; ewes often smaller horns or hornless depending on line, but powerful racing ewes remain plausible.

## 4. Coat, Coloration & Markings

Cream, charcoal, slate, russet, white, black and spotted; fleece length and horn pigmentation vary.

## 5. Temperament & Handling

Social flock animals; can be bold, territorial or steady depending on training and individual disposition.

## 6. Locomotion & Physical Impression

Canon: enormous omnivorous, social pack-oriented runner blending bear-like strength, bison-like bulk and dire-wolf-like running capacity; curved horns, thick mane, massive shoulders, strong hindquarters and cloven hooves. Canon scale: shoulder 1.8–2.3 m, body 3.5–4.5 m, 900–1,600 kg; life roughly 25–35 years.

## 3. Male and Female Appearance

Males may have larger neck mane and heavier horns; females remain massive, with somewhat cleaner neck profile. This dimorphism is proposed, not previously established.

Deep brown, black-brown, ash gray, reddish umber and pale buff, with mane and leg markings.

Highly social, pack-oriented and bonded to racing partners and handlers; individual boldness varies.

## 6. Locomotion & Physical Impression

# B09 — Sunhorn (Saelor)

## 1. Species Identity & Canon Status

**Established association:** Zarashi signature; nonexclusive. **Status:** Core species identity from national canon; named lines in Section 8 are cataloged bloodlines unless explicitly designated descriptive styles.

## 2. General Anatomy & Silhouette

Large muscular desert-adapted oryx-like antelope with long powerful legs, strong chest and hindquarters, straight or gently swept prominent horns, distinctive facial markings. No inherent magic.

## 3. Male and Female Appearance

Both sexes may be horned; males often heavier through neck and horn bases, females generally finer, subject to breeding variation.

## 4. Coat, Coloration & Markings

Ivory, sand, cream, pale gold and reddish brown, with dark facial masks, flank lines and countershading.

## 5. Temperament & Handling

Alert, watchful and responsive to patient handling; individual animals vary in confidence and tractability.

## 6. Locomotion & Physical Impression

Long efficient running stride, lifted head and economical movement on firm desert ground and suitable sand.

## 7. Visual Identification & Exclusions

## Production Standard: Named Bloodlines and Descriptive Variants

**Approved taxonomy:** `species → optional named bloodline → descriptive phenotype → individual identity`. Species and named bloodlines are persistent catalog entries; descriptive variants are reusable combinations of attributes, **not automatically new breeds**. A horse may be assigned a familiar descriptive stock style (heavy draft, compact draft, sprint racer, endurance runner, light carriage, mountain pony, desert courser) without receiving a newly invented pedigree name. Existing named Sunhorn lines remain Saelorin, Arusael, and Silsael.

**Catalog policy:** Each non-horse species has four named reference bloodlines in this edition. These are named **production-catalog entries**; all except the three previously established Sunhorn lines are newly introduced by this v1.0 reference. Their names can be edited in a future canon revision without changing species identity. No named bloodline is compulsory for an individual animal. Unregistered, mixed-line, or unspecified stock are valid and should remain common.

**Generation:** Select a species first; select a named bloodline or `unregistered` second; then generate frame, proportions, coat/hide/scale color, markings, horn/antler shape where relevant, mane/fleece, temperament, age appearance, scars, and other distinctive features within species anatomy. Bloodline sets a visual reference tendency, not an unbreakable phenotype or gameplay modifier. Preserve each animal's appearance seed and descriptor record across portraits and race scenes.

**Cross-national availability:** Stock can be imported, sold, bred, and raced beyond its nation of origin. Sundaran in particular remains an open international stock market. Cultural signature species are associations, not exclusivity rules. Teams continue to field two draft beasts under existing race rules.
